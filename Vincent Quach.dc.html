IDEAS · AUG 7, 2026

Why the Weird "Hello World"

Agnes Callard's "uni-context" is a theory that modern life and technologies has collapsed everything context-wise into a single universal "room" with one set of norms and values. For most of human history, you looked to multiple local circumstances on how to act and think. The local churches, schools, and community for example had their own set of rules, however, today we live in a universal room that's online.

Our contexts have merged largely due to platform incentives and engagement algorithms that have been ingrained into our society for profit. These technologies manufacture four major problems that affect our context: negativity, identity over character, status anxiety/homogenization, and attention crisis.

Negativity is simple: bad things are universally understood across contexts while good things are context-dependent and boring. Mrs. Smith was found dead in her home is more relatable than Mrs. Smith won a croquet tournament.

Identity over character is how everyone is judged/labeled into categories these days rather than seeking individual context-dependent character. Woman, disabled, gay, Jewish, American are easy labels to use and facilitates a uni-context world. But to truly understand someone we need to know them and experience them over time in many variety of contexts. Unfortunately because of uni-context, generalization is the preferred method to read someone, requiring little effort.

Status anxiety leading to homogenization is the societal shift as unrelated fields of life intersect. We're suddenly able to see all parts of life and feel like we're part of a global community which forces us to compare all these fields using a single homogeneous context.

The attention crisis is the recent top-down management of attention we constantly feel when we pulled in an infinite number of directions everyday. Treating attention as a resource is a modern dilemma that accentuates the need for a uni-context and a divorce from local contexts.

So what is the counter balance for uni-contextual inflation?

  • 01Treat uni-context as a means rather than an ends. Yes it has its uses but understand it is not the goal. Local context matters as much if not more.
  • 02Notice when you label someone or yourself. Instead judge people on character across different situations.
  • 03Keep uni-contextualizers (like phones) away from local spaces (like the dinner table) because it re-localizes the room.
  • 04Notice when you enter comparison fields you didn't choose especially when ranking yourself against people with context you don't see.
  • 05When posting or scrolling notice condemnation and negativity, that's uni-context talking.
  • 06In your domain of expertise, deliberately specialize rather than optimize toward the universal benchmark.
  • 07For one day a week, let your attention run bottom-up; have no plan, follow whatever comes up that interests you.
  • 08Curate feeds down to people who share your contexts.

READING

Know MyShelf.

"First Goldilocks tried the papa bear's bowl of porridge, but it was full of Brussels sprouts, which she hated."

You'll laugh. "No, that's wrong!" We'll be sitting side by side on the sofa, the skinny, overpriced hardcover spread open on our laps.

I'll keep reading. "Then Goldilocks tried the mama bear's bowl of porridge, but it was full of spinach, which she also hated."

You'll put your hand on the page of the book to stop me. "You have to read it the right way!"

"I'm reading just what it says here," I'll say, all innocence.

"No you're not. That's not how the story goes."

"Well if you already know how the story goes, why do you need me to read it to you?"

" 'Cause I wanna hear it!"
